Persistence

“I heard you’re the bravest warrior in the kingdom’s army. How come you’re so small?”

The person across from me froze for a moment. Then, in one swift motion, he drew his sword and pointed it at me. A cold flash of steel cut through the air before he sheathed it again.

He shot back jokingly,

“Then I heard you’re the most profound scholar around. How come you look and sound so young?”

“Are the stories about you not real?”

No.

They really happened.

I rolled up my trouser leg.

By the light, he could see line after line of cuts across my thigh, though most of them had already faded until they were barely visible.

Scars are a symbol of bravery, aren’t they?

“Even when you’re the one who caused them?”

…

You see, I still can’t convince myself not to kill myself.

Every day, every hour, every moment, the thought grows stronger.

So all I can do is keep hurting myself, hoping that somehow it will make the thought weaker.

He looked up at me.

“You know, I used to have a really, really good friend.”

“Then he joined the army. The expeditionary force.”

“And after that, I never heard from him again.”

So that’s why you come to this abandoned perimeter every day to practice?

“This is where we spent our last days together. He and I lived and played here for decades.”

He might have been sobbing.

He lowered his head and turned his face away.

“I can’t let go of the memories I had with him.”

“I can’t let go of this place.”

“I can’t let go of any of it.”

I patted him on the back.

Look at it this way.

At least you got to have that friendship.

At least you had all those years of happiness.

“Thanks…”

“To be honest, this is the first time I’ve ever seen you leave your place.”

Definitely not because I was planning to jump off.

Definitely not.

I sat on the wall at the edge of the perimeter, my legs dangling in the air.

It looked like about 120 blocks down.

More than enough to kill someone without armor.

“Going outside is actually pretty nice. You can meet all kinds of interesting people.”

You can do that online too.

And online, you can become the person you actually want to be.

“You…”

He looked me over carefully, his eyes lingering on my somewhat awkward-looking body.

“You look perfectly fine to me.”

I don’t hear people say things like that about me very often.

I smiled.

You have no idea how long it took me just to reach the point where I could look “fine.”

And you have no idea how much I’ll have to go through in the future just to stay this way.

In their stories, all you have to do is walk outside and somehow you’ll stumble into friends.

That’s because they-

because you-

aren’t monsters.

But I am.

Monsters don’t get the beautiful childhoods written about in books.

Monsters don’t get the friendships written about in books.

A monster can only remain a monster, with absolutely no sense of belonging anywhere.

So the monster posts his thoughts online and receives approval from people.

As long as nobody knows he’s a monster, he can comfortably pretend that he is one of them.

“I’m sorry you had to go through all that.”

He met my eyes, then looked up at the stars.

The tear tracks on his face still hadn’t dried.

Thank you.

I haven’t told anyone about my past in a very long time.

You’re the only person I’ve told in years.

You know, I’m actually kind of jealous that you can cry.

“Oh?”

He looked almost surprised that I hadn’t said crying was a sign of weakness.

I can’t cry anymore.

Not anymore.

Never again.

My tears have been boiled dry too many times.

I don’t even know why I’m sad.

The only way I know how to let it out is through anger.

I don’t really know what the source of my sadness is, either.

Except that no one ever treats me the way they would treat a normal person.

And I don’t have any way to fix it.

“Maybe asking your family for help would be a good idea?”

No.

They were the trigger for all of this.

Or maybe the cause.

No one has ever truly understood me.

I screamed in pain, over and over again, and it solved nothing.

From beginning to end, I’ve only ever had myself.

That’s why I’ve never been good at comforting people.

Hardly anyone has ever done it for me.

“It’s 4:47.”

You’re right.

We should go to sleep.

If we stay awake any longer, the phantoms might start coming.

“Do you have enough fireworks?”

“Do you want to grab some from the abandoned main storage over here?”

“Almost nobody knows there are still supplies inside. Everyone moved to other cities a long time ago.”

I guess people just like new things.

Goodbye.

It was nice talking to the bravest warrior alive.

I didn’t hear what he said in reply, because I was already sprinting toward the other end of the perimeter with my elytra on.

I fired my rockets and accelerated as fast as I could.

Just before I crashed into the wall, I pulled upward and shot into the sky.

The moonlight was beautiful.

Heh, I’ll leave my suicide for the next day.
